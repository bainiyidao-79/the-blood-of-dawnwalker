## The Setup: Europe Has Already Fallen

The Blood of Dawnwalker takes place in an **alternative 14th-century Europe** — a continent already torn apart by **war and the Black Death**. Human society is collapsing when a group of **ancient vampires** steps out of the shadows, overthrows the old feudal rulers, and takes control. Their leader, **Brencis**, is set to seal that new order with a coronation — **30 days away** when the story begins.

Brencis is not written as a cartoon villain. Under vampire rule, the people of **Vale Sangora** — a fictional Carpathian valley — are protected from the plague and freed from several feudal obligations. The trade-off: they must provide **blood** to their new rulers. Safer from disease, but effectively livestock. That moral contradiction sits at the center of the story, and Rebel Wolves has been explicit that its characters live in shades of gray rather than hero/villain camps.

## Coen's Condition: Why He's a Dawnwalker

Coen is a young man who spent years working in a **silver mine**. The silver dust left in his body — visible as a bluish-gray tint to his skin — does something crucial: it **interferes with his vampire transformation**. When he is attacked and turned, he doesn't become a normal vampire.

Instead he becomes a **Dawnwalker**:

- **By day** — mostly human: swordplay, fists, and rune magic carved into his own flesh
- **By night** — the vampire awakens: claws, blood feeding, supernatural senses, and traversal powers like Shadow Step

The silver that weakened his curse is also what makes him dangerous to the regime that made him.

## The Clock: 30 Days to Save His Family

After Brencis takes Coen's surviving family, Coen is given **30 days and nights** to rescue them and disrupt the vampire regime. The system is the game's signature:

- Time does **not** tick in real time — exploring, fighting and searching are free
- The clock advances only through **major actions**: heavy quest lines, spending skill points, certain dialogue choices, deliberate rest
- Each day and night is divided into **8 segments**, with time costs shown up front
- If the clock runs out, there is **no game-over screen** — the family is lost, the world reacts, and the story continues down a darker branch

## A Narrative Sandbox, Not a Checklist

After the prologue, the game stops dividing content into "main" and "side" quests. You have one central objective — save the family, reach Brencis's castle — and everything else is an activity you can pursue, ignore, interrupt or **destroy outright**:

- Tackle regions in any order, or abandon storylines completely
- Kill characters involved in a storyline and it changes — sometimes violently
- Skip helping someone, and they may be dead when you finally arrive
- Rush to Brencis early if you like — the devs say it's near-impossible, but it's allowed

Rebel Wolves says the game is built so that even failing quests, ignoring people, or killing important characters still leads to **an ending** — just a significantly worse one for Coen. The consequence system also runs in your favor: helping one person can mean arriving too late for another.

## The Infamy System: The Regime Fights Back

Sabotaging the vampire regime raises Coen's **notoriety** — and Brencis doesn't sit idle. As Infamy rises, the regime issues new **edicts**: additional patrols in settlements, more military checkpoints, even higher prices at shopkeepers. Brencis's lieutenants — the officers running the valley's dominions — can personally come after you, opening new storylines of their own. Every activity is optional, but almost all of them cost time or notoriety. That's the deal.
