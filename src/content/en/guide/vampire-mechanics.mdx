## The Core Loop: Two Coens, One Body

The Blood of Dawnwalker's defining mechanic is Coen's split nature — and the two forms play almost like different classes:

- **By day**, Coen is mostly human: he fights with **swords and fists**, talks his way through villages, and casts **rune magic**
- **By night**, the vampire awakens: **razor-sharp claws**, **Shadow Step** (short teleports), **Shadow Walk** (moving across walls and ceilings), supernatural senses, blood feeding — and eventually **wolf transformation** for fast travel

Some areas are only naturally reachable in one form. Watchtowers that reveal the map, for example, need Coen's night-time movement to climb — find one during the day and you either come back later or spend time advancing the clock.

## Day: The Human

Daytime Coen has **no passive healing** — he leans on potions, food and consumables. What he gets instead is exclusive access to **rune magic**: spells carved as runes into his own flesh.

- Hexes can ignite Coen's sword, tangle enemies in thorny vines, or shield him
- One spell lets him **speak with the dead** — opening information and quest branches
- They're **sacrificial spells**: casting costs Coen his own health

Why day-only? Because at night, Coen's vampire regeneration heals those rune carvings right back — the wounds that channel his magic don't exist on the vampire. It's a clever anti-favoritism lever: nighttime is stronger for movement and raw power, but daytime owns social access, swordplay and investigation.

## Night: The Vampire

Nighttime is power and predation — with one big catch. Coen's **health bar doubles as a hunger gauge**:

- **Feeding** restores health: bite enemies in combat, drain animals (less efficient), or approach innocents
- **Refusing to feed** has consequences — as hunger deepens, Coen loses his grip and can slip into a **feral frenzy**, attacking the nearest person mid-dialogue. Previewers watched a saved NPC become Coen's next meal because the conversation ran long on an empty stomach
- Every NPC is potentially killable, so a frenzy can permanently erase quest givers

Feeding also raises Coen's **corruption level**, which gates access to increasingly strong vampire perks (see progression below).

## Combat: Directional Swordplay

Combat uses a **directional system** closer to Kingdom Come: Deliverance than to The Witcher 3:

- Attacks come from **up, down, left or right** to bypass an enemy's guard
- Defend by **matching the incoming direction** — a correct timed block becomes a parry, and **perfect parries cost no stamina** while staggering the enemy
- Regular attacks, blocks and dodges consume stamina
- Enemies **adapt** if you repeatedly attack from the same angle; you can grab defensive enemies to break their stance
- Don't like the micro-management? **Omni-block** automatically picks the correct direction at the cost of higher stamina drain — and auto-block can be toggled separately in the settings

## Skill Trees & Progression

Coen's progression splits into **three trees plus general upgrades**:

| Tree | What it builds | How it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| **Sword Mastery** | Weapon techniques, defense | Techniques learned from **manuals** found in the world |
| **Witchcraft** | Ritual magic (day) | **Haunted sites** — day-only trials fought with blood magic, rewarding manuals |
| **Vampirism** | Claws, traversal, wolf form | **Corruption level** — raised by feeding |
| **General** | Health, stamina, carrying capacity | Standard upgrades |

Three wrinkles make it strategic:

- Perks are purchased with XP **at shrines** — which are also fast-travel and rest points
- **Spending skill points consumes in-game time** — training is an expense, not a free reward
- Each tree has an ultimate ability, but you can only **select one ultimate** total

## Difficulty Options

Presets run from **Story** to **Duelist** (no directional indicators, less enemy HP but far more aggressive enemies, less stamina for Coen). Rebel Wolves also exposes **granular sliders** — enemy HP, damage, aggression, Coen's stamina and more can be tuned individually — plus damage-indicator and camera (far/near) options.

## The Infamy System: Your Enemies Adapt

Sabotaging the regime raises **notoriety**, and Brencis responds with new **edicts**: extra patrols, more checkpoints, higher shop prices. His lieutenants can open new storylines targeting you. The world doesn't wait — it escalates.
